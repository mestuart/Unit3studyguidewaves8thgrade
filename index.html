Mechanical waves and transverse waves

A mechanical wave needs a medium (matter) to travel through. Sound, water waves, waves on a rope and earthquake waves are mechanical waves. They cannot travel through empty space.

A transverse wave is a wave in which the particles of the medium move perpendicular (at a right angle) to the direction the wave travels. Waves on a rope or string and ripples on water are transverse. Light and all other electromagnetic waves are transverse too.

Need a medium?YesNo, they can cross empty space
ExamplesSound, rope waves, water wavesLight, radio, X-rays

Waves move energy, not matter

A wave transfers energy from one place to another. The matter of the medium does not travel along with the wave. Each particle only moves back and forth around its resting point, and then ends up where it started.

  • Shake a rope: the energy travels down the rope, but the rope stays in your hands.
  • A cork on a pond bobs up and down as waves pass. It is not carried to shore by the wave.
  • A stadium wave moves around the stadium, but each fan only stands up and sits down in the same seat.

Parts of a transverse wave

A wave carries energy from one place to another. It does not carry matter along with it. In a transverse wave, the particles move up and down while the wave travels sideways. The two motions are perpendicular, which means at a right angle.

Explain the relationships

1. Explain the relationship between frequency and wavelength.

Sentence starters: As the frequency of a wave increases, the wavelength ___. This is because ___.

Show a sample answer

Frequency and wavelength have an inverse relationship. When waves travel at the same speed, a higher frequency means a shorter wavelength, and a lower frequency means a longer wavelength. More waves pass a point each second, so each wave has to be shorter to fit. For example, X-rays have a higher frequency and a much shorter wavelength than radio waves.

2. Explain the relationship between amplitude and frequency.

Sentence starters: Amplitude and frequency are ___ of each other. Changing the amplitude makes the wave ___, but it does not change ___.

Show a sample answer

Amplitude and frequency are independent. They do not depend on each other. Amplitude is how far the wave rises from the resting point, and it is linked to the energy of the wave. Frequency is how many waves pass a point each second. On a rope, shaking your hand with a bigger motion changes the amplitude, and shaking it faster changes the frequency. You can change one without changing the other.

Patterns to know

  • Shake a rope with a bigger motion and the amplitude goes up. The wave carries more energy.
  • Shake faster and the frequency goes up. More waves pass each second.
  • When waves travel at the same speed, a higher frequency means a shorter wavelength. Frequency and wavelength change in opposite directions.
  • All electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light in empty space, about 300,000 km per second.

The electromagnetic spectrum (8.8A)

Electromagnetic (EM) waves are transverse waves that do not need a medium. They can travel through empty space. The spectrum lists them by wavelength and frequency.

How we use EM waves (8.8B)

Radiation therapy

Doctors aim a narrow beam of high-energy X-rays or gamma rays at a tumor. The energy damages the cancer cells so they stop growing. The beam is aimed from several angles to protect healthy tissue.

Wireless technologies

Phones, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, radio and TV use radio waves and microwaves. A device turns information into waves, and another device turns the waves back into information. No wire is needed because EM waves travel through air and space.

Fiber optics

Data is sent as pulses of light (often infrared) through thin strands of glass. The light bounces along inside the glass, even around bends, so a signal can travel a long way with very little loss.

Microwaves

A microwave oven sends microwaves into food. Water molecules in the food absorb the energy and vibrate, which warms the food. Microwaves are also used for radar and satellite communication.

Ultraviolet sterilization

Ultraviolet (UV) light has enough energy to damage the DNA of germs, so they cannot reproduce. Hospitals and water-treatment plants use UV lamps to sterilize tools, air and water.

Astronomical observations

Stars, galaxies and gas clouds give off many kinds of EM waves. Radio, infrared, visible, ultraviolet and X-ray telescopes each reveal different details. Putting the pictures together gives a fuller view of the universe.

X-rays

X-rays pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by dense bone. A detector behind the body records the difference, so bones show up white. X-rays are also used in airport security scanners.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Amplitude is measured from the resting point to a crest. It is not the full distance from trough to crest.
  • Wavelength is a distance along the wave. Frequency is a count of waves per second. Do not mix them up.
  • Higher frequency means a shorter wavelength, not a longer one.
  • Mechanical waves need a medium. Electromagnetic waves do not. Both can be transverse waves.
  • A wave moves energy, not matter. The particles of the medium do not travel with the wave.
  • In a transverse wave, the particles move perpendicular to the way the wave travels, not in the same direction.
  • Visible light is only a small part of the EM spectrum. Radio waves, X-rays and the others are EM waves too.
  • Microwaves, radio waves and X-rays all travel at the same speed in empty space. They differ in wavelength, frequency and energy.
